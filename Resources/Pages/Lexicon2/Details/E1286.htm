further
/fer-ther/
 
adj, adv.
 
1.
Def.: diyè dapit (meyitenged te kariyuan te sabeka ne lugar puun te sabeka); madmariyù
MS: diyè dapit; madmariyù
You use further to compare distances. If one thing is further from a place than another thing, the distance is bigger.
I walk further than my friend.
Eggipanew a diyè pad dapit te alukuy ku.
I live further from the school than my friend, so it takes longer for me to walk there.
Irreg. adj. & adv.  far, further, furthest.
 
adv.
 
2.
Def.: pabulus-bulus
MS: pabulus-bulus
If you do something further, you continue to do it, or do it more.
I'm tired, and I can't walk any further.
Nabeley ad, wey kenè ad eggipanew pabulus-bulus.
She didn't answer his questions clearly, so he questioned her further.
I'd like to talk with you further about this.
 
adj.
 
3.
Def.: duma pad
MS: duma pad
If there are further things, there are more of them remaining.
I have finished my work, and there is no further work to do.
Neimpusan kud ka trabahu ku, warad duma pad ne eggimuwen ku.
After we fixed the roof we had no further problems with rain.
He would like to do further study after he finishes high school.
 
vt.
 
4.
Def.: egparakelen; egpameupiyeen
MS: egparakelen; egpameupiyeen
If you further something, you help it to become bigger or better.
He went to the night classes to further his chances of getting a job.
Eg-iskuyla sikandin te marusilem eyew te pegparakel te timpu rin ne egpakakuwa te trabahu.
He is furthering his education in Manila.
 
adv.
 
5.
Def.: wey labet pad maa
MS: wey labet pad maa
You use further to add extra information which makes something stronger.
She closed the door and, further, put a 'do not disturb' sign on it.
Inlekeban din ka gemawan, wey labet pad maa migtahù sikandin te kanengnengan ne warè egsamuk.
Source: adverbs; GSL0476-0500
