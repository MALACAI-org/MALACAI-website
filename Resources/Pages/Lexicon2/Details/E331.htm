bill1
 
n-ct.
 
1.
Def.: bayaranen
MS: bayaranen
a piece of paper which shows how much money you need to pay for something (e.g., an electricity bill).
We get an electricity bill every month.
 
vt.
 
2.
Def.: egpabayad
MS: egpabayad
If someone bills you, they ask you to pay for something. They usually give you a piece of paper which shows how much you need to pay.
The builder billed us for his work on our house.
 
n-ct.
 
3.
Def.: hangyù te pegbalew-balew te balaud
MS: hangyù te pegbalew-balew te balaud
In a government law-making group, a bill is a formal request to change something in the law.
The politicians talked about a bill to make cigarettes more expensive.
 
n-ct.
 
4.
Def.: seleppì ne pepil
MS: seleppì ne pepil
In America and Canada, a bill is a piece of paper money (e.g. a twenty-dollar bill).
Sorry, I don't have any coins, only a fifty-peso bill.
 
n-ct.
 
5.
Def.: listahan te me etew ne egkunsirt wey ke listahan te minsan nekey ne igpakita ran
a list of people who will perform at a concert, or a list of the things that they will perform.
There are several famous singers on the bill for that concert.
 
vt.
 
6.
Def.: igpanengneng; igpapeila-ila
MS: igpanengneng; igpapeila-ila
If you bill something as a certain thing, you advertise it so that people will think it is that thing.
The advertisers billed the concert as 'the best concert of the year'.
Ka egmapeila-ila migpanengneng te ka kunsirt 'iyan kinameupiyaan ne kunsirt te tuig'.
 
n-ct.
 
7.
Def.: pepil te pegpanengneng
MS: pepil te pegpanengneng
a piece of paper with an advertisement or notice printed on it.
You are not allowed to post bills on that wall.
Source: GSL0751-0775
