object1
/ob-djekt/
 
n-ct.
 
1.
Def.: kasangkapan; butang
MS: kasangkapan; butang
a thing
His motorbike hit a large object on the road.
Ka mutur din nakasukeung te dakel ne butang diyè te dalan.
 
n-ct.
 
2.
Def.: ka egkakita wey egkabehayan te panahun
MS: egkakita; egkabehayan
The object of your emotion or action is the person or thing that you feel the emotion about, or do the action to.
You are the object of his love.
Sikeykew ka egkabehayan te eggeramen din ne geyinawa.
 
n-ct.
 
3.
Def.: Te Inglis ne pegkalalag, ka egguhuren te weil ne lalag iyan ka etew wey ke butang ne diyè nakatahù te impus te weil ne lalag.
In English grammar, the object of a verb is the person or thing which is after the verb. The object receives or experiences the action of the verb.
My brother hates bread ('bread' is the object of the verb 'hate').
Ka suled ku egkeepes te paan [pa͡an] (ka paan [pa͡an] ka egguhuren te weil ne lalag 'egkeepes')
See direct object, indirect object
 
n-ct.
 
4.
Def.: ka igkeupii te eg-uma wey ke egkuen
MS: eg-am-amesen; egpanganduyen
Your object is the thing that you want to achieve.
My object is to find a job.
Ka igkeupii ku ka egpamitew te trabahu.
Source: grammar; GSL0726-0750
