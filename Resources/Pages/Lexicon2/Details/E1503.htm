impose
/im-poze/
 
vt.
 
1.
Def.: egpehes ne egpeyimu
MS: egpehes
If you impose something on someone, you force them to do or suffer that thing. If you impose your thinking on someone, you try to force them to think or behave the same way as you.
The owner of the house imposed a lot of rules on the students who were living there.
Ka kamuney te baley migpehes te pegpeyimu te masulug ne balaud te me istudyanti ne nangugpè.
The bank imposed many fees on its members.
 
vi.
 
2.
Def.: egpamehes (meyitenged te pegpeyimu)
Note: I'm not sure if this translation is correct - it doesn't have an idea of forcing. It's more like rudeness - for example, if someone always comes to your house every day and expects that you will give them dinner.
MS: egpamehes
If you impose on someone, you cause them to do things for you, even though you shouldn't ask them to do those things.
Don't visit your neighbor too often, because they might feel that you are imposing on them.
Kenè ka layun egpanumbaley te sumbaley nu, su kema ke egpakakahi sikandan te egpamehes ka kandan.
My friends invited me to stay with them, but I don't want to impose on them, so I'm going to stay with my uncle instead.
I feel that she really imposes on me. She always expects me to drive her everywhere.
Source: AWL4
 
 
imposition
/im-pə-zish-ən/
 
n-uc.
Def.: 1 pegpehes; 2 pegpamehes
MS: pegpehes; pegpamehes
the action of imposing.
 
n-ct.
Def.: pegpamehes
MS: pegpamehes
something which someone asks you to do for them, even though they shouldn't ask.
They expected us to pay for everything! That was an imposition.
